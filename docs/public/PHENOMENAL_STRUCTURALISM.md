# Phenomenal structuralism: comparisons and objections

**Status: philosophical comparison (HEUR); substantive questions marked OPEN.** No consciousness theory, mathematical result or core premise is added. SS is the separate commitment stated in [OVERVIEW](OVERVIEW.md); these authors do not jointly endorse it. Full references and primary-text links appear once in the [bibliography](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#phenomenal-structuralism).

## Different commitments

**Lyre (2022)** proposes relational individuation of specific phenomenal character and a surjective homomorphism from neural to phenomenal structure. In “Phenomenality, content, and characters,” he separates specificity from the general mechanism making content conscious. His Newman response appeals to spatiotemporal grounding and sensory intersections. “Reductive physicalism” adds metaphysically necessary neural–phenomenal supervenience; Lyre acknowledges that this necessity is a commitment of his physicalist programme rather than an independently established argument. SS shares the rejection of an independent phenomenal remainder, but does not adopt these neural, spatiotemporal or reductive premises. It begins with experienced differences, without requiring their derivation from a separately given nonphenomenal domain.

**Kleiner (2024), §§3–5,** distinguishes using mathematical structures from structuralist individuation. Structural methods alone carry no preferred metaphysics. A bare isomorphism presupposes structures on both sides and does not select or explain the phenomenal structure. His quality-space discussion separates experiential content, laboratory operations and mathematical convenience. This supports keeping model correspondence, attribution and explanation distinct. SS adds a philosophical commitment beyond that methodological position; Q-factorization does not establish it. Neither Kleiner’s individuation criterion nor a demand for trivial automorphisms is adopted as an SS axiom.

**Isaac (2025), §§2–5,** connects quality-space geometry, constancies and valence/affordance with meaning and action. Structure-preserving correspondences guide iterative model refinement as a regulative ideal. His “isomorphism” is methodologically broader than a strict mathematical isomorphism; introspective checking and partial convergence between methods support targeting experience. He expressly leaves isolated pleasure/pain unsettled (§3) and ends with the problem of averaging away individual experience (§6). These are resources for investigating particular attributions, not a proof of SS or mandatory conditions for every felt distinction. His programme and Kleiner’s critique assign different explanatory roles to correspondences; calling both structural does not resolve that disagreement.

## Negro’s three arguments

**Negro (2025), §§2–4,** targets metaphysical exhaustion by relations to other experiences, while retaining methodological and epistemological structuralism. The following responses concern SS’s broader substantive organization, not a proof that his target view succeeds.

**Relational Mary (§3.1).** Mary knows colour relations and other colours but first encounters orange. Negro considers the descriptive/experiential knowledge reply and argues that it fails to vindicate relational priority. For SS, a description of a relation and acquiring access to it need not have identical epistemic consequences. That distinction does not automatically defeat his argument: the contested inference is from a gain in knowledge to a fact independent of substantive organization. Access and acquaintance are themselves substantive organization, not merely reports. **OPEN:** investigate the particular knowledge Mary acquires, including its propositional claims and conditions of access, and assess Negro's argument for a fact independent of that organization.

**Novel Experience (§3.2).** Enlarging the experiential repertoire allegedly changes every prior experience if its character depends on the whole network. His intervention case includes newly acquired capacities, not just first use of an old capacity.

**KNOWN / NEGATIVE — universal inference:** enlarging structural organization alone does not entail a change in every prior local comparison; that conclusion requires an additional sensitivity or global-individuation condition. Conversely, preserving selected comparisons does not entail complete phenomenal identity. These are known logical and mathematical limits on inference, not a new theorem, evidence for SS or a refutation of Negro’s argument as a whole. SS neither makes global dependencies fundamental nor excludes them when substantively justified within a concrete theory.

**OPEN / EMPIRICAL — stability of experienced differences:** which differences remain stable when organization, repertoire or access changes, and how could their preservation be established? SS alone does not settle this substantive question. Articulating or gaining access to a distinction does not by itself create it. Matching reports or formally similar comparison tables do not establish phenomenal identity.

**Lonely Experience (§3.3).** A conceivable single-experience system challenges the requirement of at least two related experiences. Negro sets aside bare self-identity and sketches a one-smell workspace. Absence of other experiences is not absence of all relations: internal organization, dependencies and access conditions are candidates for investigation. Merely listing them does not establish their phenomenal relevance. SS cannot reject isolated experience by definition or infer consciousness from a workspace diagram. **OPEN:** which relations, if any, determine character in the proposed case, and what grounds that attribution?

## What remains at issue

Structural methods can succeed without metaphysical SS. Conversely, weaknesses in a particular similarity network do not by themselves establish a nonstructural ground of experience. The substantive dispute concerns what relations obtain and whether an independent ontological addition explains anything further. New relations are legitimate theory extensions; a remainder at fixed substantive organization is excluded conditionally under SS. None of these comparisons turns an access difference, an isomorphism or a failed Q-test into a verdict on the competing metaphysics.
