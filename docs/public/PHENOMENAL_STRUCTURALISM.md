# Phenomenal structuralism: comparisons and objections

**Status: philosophical comparison (HEUR); substantive questions marked OPEN.** No consciousness theory, mathematical result or core premise is added. SS is the separate commitment stated in [OVERVIEW](OVERVIEW.md); these authors do not jointly endorse it. Full references and primary-text links appear once in the [bibliography](BIBLIOGRAPHY.md#phenomenal-structuralism).

## Different commitments

**Lyre (2022)** proposes relational individuation of specific phenomenal character and a surjective homomorphism from neural to phenomenal structure. In “Phenomenality, content, and characters,” he separates specificity from the general mechanism making content conscious. His Newman response appeals to spatiotemporal grounding and sensory intersections. “Reductive physicalism” adds metaphysically necessary neural–phenomenal supervenience; Lyre acknowledges that this necessity is a commitment of his physicalist programme rather than an independently established argument. SS shares the rejection of an independent phenomenal remainder, but does not adopt these neural, spatiotemporal or reductive premises. It begins with differences given in experience, without requiring their derivation from a separately stipulated physical or nonphenomenal basis. This experiential primacy does not divide relations into two ontological kinds or make every relation phenomenal. The authors' terms below identify their explanatory commitments; they are not primitive categories of SS.

**Kleiner (2024), §§3–5,** distinguishes using mathematical structures from structuralist individuation. Structural methods alone carry no preferred metaphysics. A bare isomorphism presupposes structures on both sides and does not select or explain the phenomenal structure. His quality-space discussion separates experiential content, laboratory operations and mathematical convenience. This supports keeping model correspondence, attribution and explanation distinct. SS adds a philosophical commitment beyond that methodological position; Q-factorization does not establish it. Neither Kleiner’s individuation criterion nor a demand for trivial automorphisms is adopted as an SS axiom.

**Isaac (2025), §§2–5,** connects quality-space geometry, constancies and valence/affordance with meaning and action. Structure-preserving correspondences guide iterative model refinement as a regulative ideal. His “isomorphism” is methodologically broader than a strict mathematical isomorphism; introspective checking and partial convergence between methods support targeting experience. He expressly leaves isolated pleasure/pain unsettled (§3) and ends with the problem of averaging away individual experience (§6). These are resources for investigating particular attributions, not a proof of SS or mandatory conditions for every felt distinction. His programme and Kleiner’s critique assign different explanatory roles to correspondences; calling both structural does not resolve that disagreement.

## Negro’s three arguments

**Negro (2025), §§2–4,** targets metaphysical exhaustion by relations to other experiences, while retaining methodological and epistemological structuralism. The following responses concern SS’s broader substantive organization, not a proof that his target view succeeds.

**Relational Mary (§3.1).** Mary knows colour relations and other colours but first encounters orange. Negro considers the descriptive/experiential knowledge reply and argues that it fails to vindicate relational priority. For SS, knowing a description need not reproduce what it describes. First encountering orange can involve genuinely new distinctions, comparisons and relations, including propositional knowledge; it need not be merely new access to a previously complete organization. His counter-reply concerns metaphysical priority, rather than merely distinguishing a description from an encounter. Conditions of acquiring knowledge do not by themselves establish that metaphysical direction; SS does not assume relations to be absolutely prior to relata either. Calling the acquisition organization supplies no sufficient explanation. **HEUR — philosophical limit:** neither the gain alone nor its acquisition order establishes a nonrelational ground or a contradiction with SS. **OPEN — concrete task:** what is acquired and how its particular comparisons and composition are explained. **External challenge:** an independently defended argument for nonrelational constitution remains a challenge to SS; it is not a standing internal demand to add phenomenality. Negro is not declared refuted.

**Novel Experience (§3.2).** Enlarging the experiential repertoire allegedly changes every prior experience if its character depends on the whole network. His intervention case includes newly acquired capacities, not just first use of an old capacity.

**KNOWN / NEGATIVE — universal inference:** enlarging structural organization alone does not entail a change in every prior local comparison; that conclusion requires an additional sensitivity or global-individuation condition. Conversely, preserving selected comparisons establishes preservation only of those comparisons; composition, adaptation or newly available comparisons may still differ. It establishes no identity of the entire content under inquiry and creates no further requirement for complete experiential identity beyond substantively justified contrasts. These are known logical and mathematical limits on inference, not a new theorem, evidence for SS or a refutation of Negro’s argument as a whole. SS neither makes global dependencies fundamental nor excludes them when substantively justified within a concrete theory.

**OPEN / EMPIRICAL — stability of experienced differences:** which differences remain stable when organization, repertoire or access changes, and how could their preservation be established? SS alone does not settle this substantive question. Articulating or gaining access to a distinction does not by itself create it. Matching reports or formally similar comparison tables do not establish preservation of every substantive difference. The empirical task must name what is compared; its incompleteness does not reopen a separate general metaphysical debt within SS.

**Lonely Experience (§3.3).** A conceivable single-experience system challenges the requirement of at least two related experiences. Negro sets aside bare self-identity and sketches a one-smell workspace. One smell type, one sensory channel, one operation, one episode and one classification result are different counting claims. Abstraction to one smell type does not remove the sensor, memory, attention, broadcast and feedback relations explicitly represented in his conditional workspace example. Nor does their listing establish that they explain the particular smell. SS requires neither two different experiences nor an additional account of what makes already explained relations phenomenal. It cannot reject a qualitatively sparse case by definition or infer experience from a diagram. **HEUR — philosophical limit:** one classified content establishes no absence of substantive relations; conceivability and the assumed workspace theory do not by themselves establish physical realization. **OPEN — concrete task:** justify the counting unit and explain the particular content and dependencies claimed for the case. **External challenge:** an argument that these require additional nonrelational constitution remains assessable; the word experience alone does not establish that requirement.

## What remains at issue

Structural methods can succeed without metaphysical SS. Conversely, weaknesses in a particular similarity network do not by themselves establish a nonstructural ground of experience. The substantive dispute concerns what relations obtain and whether a proposed additional ontological ground has independently defended explanatory work. A necessary nonrelational constitution can challenge SS without freely varying qualities; successful Q-factorization would not decide that dispute. Concrete constitutive direction can instead be a substantive dependence, without absolute fundamentality. New relations are legitimate theory extensions; a remainder at fixed substantive organization is excluded conditionally under SS. None of these comparisons turns an access difference, an isomorphism or a failed Q-test into a verdict on the competing metaphysics.
