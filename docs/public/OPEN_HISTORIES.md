# SS and open histories

**Status: interpretive clarification, with conditional formal results.** Adopting Structural Sufficiency does not establish an absolutely unique history. Uniqueness requires structural grounds of its own. Multiple historical organizations are admissible when substantive relations distinguish them; this is not a proof of many worlds.

## The argument

1. SS rejects an additional fact that varies independently of substantive organization. The formal Q test checks the narrower question of whether a specified fact is determined by a stated theory.
2. Absolute historical uniqueness asserts that there is only one history, rather than multiple historical organizations. SS does not establish this claim; it requires structural grounds of its own. A privileged history is a different claim: one history is distinguished from others by a specified criterion or selector. Rejecting an ungrounded selector does not by itself settle absolute historical uniqueness.
3. Withholding that claim of absolute historical uniqueness does not require a proof of many worlds or objective probabilities of branches. Conversely, a positive claim that several organizations exist still needs substantive grounds. Failure to prove uniqueness proves neither plurality nor its negation.
4. Nothing here defines an objective domain of all worlds, their number, absolute boundaries or probabilities of existence. Open means that no exhaustive completion is postulated; it does not mean infinite or perpetually increasing multiplicity.

A unique answer to a specified question, a unique empirical class and an absolutely unique history are different claims. For example, two raw descriptions can have identical records; that does not select one description, establish two worlds, or prove that no richer comparison can distinguish them. No common comparison is assumed for every pair of organizations.

## Questions about concrete historical individuality

The project can ask whether a difference is discriminated, retained in records, usable through articulation, and supported by independently formed channels with a usable comparison. These are task-specific questions, not universal conditions for individuality. SS as interpreted here supplies no universal checklist or necessary sequence of stages. A claimed property needs evidence in its particular setting; absence of that evidence is not proof of absent individuality. An instrument can articulate a difference; human recognition is not required. Records need not be permanent, and a transient distinction need not satisfy a universal stability threshold to count as a distinction.

Two independent probes can both report the first bit of a two-bit record: their agreement does not recover the second bit. Two probes reading different bits can jointly distinguish all four supplied records if their outputs are jointly usable. A process resetting both bits then erases those distinctions, even though its update is perfectly well-defined. Thus discrimination, independent checking and retention are separate obligations.

This gives a conditional account of historical individuality, not a definition of indivisible worlds. It imposes no predetermined system boundary, mandatory channel count, universal duration or human articulation. Processes may merge or recur; a branching tree is not assumed. Time and causal history must be reconstructed, where justified, from represented relations, processes and records. Mere relational structure does not guarantee either reconstruction: cycles obstruct a strict order, and identical records can fit different causal accounts.

Quantum uncertainty alone supplies no branch-producing operation. In a quantum application, claims about enduring alternative records require a specified quantum model and its dynamics. The present argument neither derives those premises nor changes quantum predictions.

## Scientific neighbours and differences

These are comparisons of explanatory roles, not equivalence claims. Quantum structures in the cited theories are external premises, never imported into the Q-free core.

| Approach | Relevant connection | Limit of the comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Everett, relative states | Measurement records and correlations are central to a no-collapse account | Everett starts with wave mechanics; SS does not derive unitary dynamics or the universal wavefunction. See [Everett (1957)](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.29.454). |
| Wallace, emergent worlds | Macroscopic patterns can be substantive without exact world boundaries written into the formalism | Wallace already resists demanding a fundamental precise list of worlds. This project does not claim that insight as new; it also does not adopt his quantum ontology. See [Worlds in the Everett interpretation (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0103092) and [Everett and Structure (2003)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0107144). |
| Rovelli, relational quantum mechanics | Physical descriptions are relational; a special conscious observer is unnecessary | RQM is a quantum interpretation with physical postulates. Relational vocabulary alone neither derives it nor makes it a many-worlds theory. See [Rovelli (1996)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9609002). |
| Consistent/decoherent histories | Histories, records and compatibility of descriptions receive explicit mathematical treatment | Consistency conditions and probabilities are supplied within quantum frameworks. Incompatible frameworks cannot simply be combined into one history or reinterpreted as simultaneous worlds. See [Griffiths (1996)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9606004) and [Hartle (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04145). |
| Quantum Darwinism | Redundant environmental records offer a concrete mechanism for robust accessible distinctions | The quantum system/environment structure is supplied. Redundancy is not guaranteed for arbitrary states and is not the same as absolute independence of formation. See [Blume-Kohout and Zurek (2006)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0505031). |

A useful adverse result is [Dowker and Kent, Properties of Consistent Histories (1995)](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9409037): consistency alone does not recover the full quasiclassical inferential structure. This supports caution about a consistency-only criterion; it is not a proof that the histories programme fails. Griffiths explicitly restricts joint reasoning to compatible frameworks. The present project similarly requires a justified interface before combining records, but its ordinary finite countermodels are not quantum contextuality theorems.

## Established, proposed and open

**Established conditional mathematics:** fibre constancy determines whether a target descends; record sufficiency, exact update and preservation of a past contrast require different conditions. Equal records need not determine provenance or a causal reconstruction. Duplicating representations does not multiply empirical classes. These are applications of existing mathematics.

**Interpretation under SS:** absolute uniqueness is an additional claim requiring grounds; open historical multiplicity is admissible, not established as a world ontology. A scoped structural uniqueness theorem remains legitimate.

**Research hypothesis:** independently formed, jointly usable and retained records may stabilize specific historical distinctions under declared perturbations. Copied-channel, redundant-probe and erasure controls prevent promoting this into a universal theorem.

**Open:** independently justified causal and temporal reconstruction, empirical criteria for retention across changing repertoires, and an application producing content beyond the existing record/control framework. No theorem-level novelty, new physics, Born-rule derivation or measurement-problem solution is claimed. Universal criteria of individuality are outside this interpretation of SS. The negative research result is narrower: this pass adds no formal content beyond existing factorization, control and constraint tools.
