## Q-exclusion and philosophical SS

The shared heading “Q-Exclusion / Structural Sufficiency” in 01, section 2, labels a theory-relative factorization criterion. It does not prove philosophical SS. Factors are unique on the occupied image of the forgetful map; unused base labels receive no canonical values. Its listed exclusions concern undeclared additions to the stated core, not a proof that enriched theories are inconsistent.

Philosophical SS is the separate commitment against additional ontological determinacy beyond substantive organization of differences and relations, including phenomenal character. It requires no complete account established independently of experience. A perspective denotes local operational access and supplies no consciousness predicate. These statements interpret the scope of the frozen formulas; no formula or premise is amended here.

## Scope of empirical descent (§6)

**Scope clarification, not a new theorem.** In 01 §6, “scientist-level meaningful” concerns whether the specified relation or operation is well-defined on the chosen empirical quotient, with its declared input and output equivalences. Failed descent means it is not defined on that quotient. It is not a universal criterion of scientific meaningfulness, substantive content or admissible inquiry.

Representation-dependent or covariant quantities can have a scientific role when their transformation rules and interpretation are specified. Such a quantity need not descend as a scalar-valued function on an unchanged quotient; its appropriate typing, transformation or relational use requires separate articulation. This observation adds no formal construction to the core. Nor does a missing local test erase a primary felt difference. Descent, scientific use and grounds for attribution answer different questions.
